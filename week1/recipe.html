Ceviche

ingredients:

  • a lot of limes
  • one tomato, or maybe a handful of baby tomatoes
  • one onion
  • white fish, no bones & cut thinly. similar amount of fish as you have onion + tomato
  • jalapeno or serrano, as much as you want
  • cilantro
  • chips or tostada
  1. Cut your fish into small chunks. You can also smash it and have more of a crudo thing but in that case I wouldn't reccomend mixing it into a bowl with all the other ingredients because it would fall apart in the bowl and then you'd have wisps of fish with no bite. With small chunks, the fish is able to keep its intended texture and whatnot. The crudo route is also pretty cool if you're feeling fancy, and can use the same ingredients. Just make sure you are setting the fish apart from the rest of the ingredients.
  2. Squeeze limes into your fish - enough to cover the fish. This "cooks" it... sort of. It denatures proteins, changing the texture a little bit. This doesn't add much to food safety, though it's better than nothing. From what I understand, frozen and farmed fish are going to be preferrable for minimizing parasite risk. It's a bit of a risky dish regardless. I've always been fine. Mix the fish up in the bowl every so often to make sure the lime coats everything.
  3. Spice has a similarly small-but-not-nothing food safety bonus. Ideally, I think that peppers should be crushed in a molcajete because it can get the oils out and break the seeds up nicely. I use the lemon juicer on chunks of jalapeno. I'm not sure if it makes a positive impact. Whatever you do, the peppers can go in the fish/limem mix.
  4. Chop up the onions and tomatoes into cubes, get the cilantro diced. While doing so, mix the fish to coat it. And then everything can be tossed into a the same bowl. Hot sauce... tapatio? is pretty good as an addition.
